On 13 October 2026, I attended an interesting session about the **European AI Act**, presented by [Olivier Sustronck](https://www.linkedin.com/in/olivier-sustronck-1955b4119/).

As artificial intelligence becomes increasingly integrated into software applications and business processes, understanding the legislation surrounding it is becoming more important for developers and organisations alike.

![European AI Act presentation at Howest]({{ '/assets/images/Europena_Ai_Act.jpg' | relative_url }})

*An impression of a session about the European AI Act.*

## What did I learn?

During the session, we explored the rules governing the development and use of AI systems in Europe. One of the key topics was **high-risk AI systems**: when an AI application falls into this category and what that means for the people and organisations developing or deploying it.

We also discussed what organisations need to consider to comply with the AI Act, including their responsibilities when introducing AI systems. This highlighted that building an AI application involves more than just making it work technically. Developers and organisations must also consider the legal requirements and potential impact of the systems they create.

## Why is this relevant to software engineering?

As a Software Engineering student at Howest, I found it valuable to learn how legislation and software development intersect.

AI tools offer many opportunities, but using them responsibly also requires an understanding of the rules that apply. For future software engineers, this means considering compliance and risk alongside functionality, security, reliability and user experience.

The session gave me a better understanding of why awareness of the AI Act is relevant when developing or integrating AI into software projects.

## Key takeaway

My main takeaway is that **responsible AI development goes beyond the technology itself**. Understanding the applicable legislation is an important part of building software that can be used responsibly and in accordance with the rules.

Thank you to [Olivier Sustronck](https://www.linkedin.com/in/olivier-sustronck-1955b4119/) for the clear and practical explanation.
